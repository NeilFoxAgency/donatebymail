# Nonprofit campaign setup: a plain-language guide

This guide is for nonprofit directors, fundraisers, and communications staff who
are creating a first Donate by Mail campaign. You do not need to know HTML,
coding, or device processing. The workspace saves a private draft while you
work; a campaign is not public until the exact revision passes partner and
Donate by Mail staff review.

## Before you begin

Have these three things nearby:

- the name of the nonprofit or program the campaign supports;
- one sentence about why collecting phones helps your community; and
- a rough idea of who you want to invite (members, families, volunteers,
  alumni, event attendees, or another group).

You can leave the phone goal, dates, images, and promotion copy for later. It is
better to start with accurate, simple language than to wait for a perfect
campaign package.

## The four-question setup

1. **Name it.** Choose a working name such as “Spring phone drive.” The
   workspace creates a short web link and checks whether it is available.
2. **Choose support.** Select the verified nonprofit beneficiary. If it does
   not appear, stop and ask your Donate by Mail contact to complete
   verification; do not choose a similar-looking organization.
3. **Tell the story.** Write a headline, a one- or two-sentence explanation,
   and a few sentences about who the campaign helps. Write as if you were
   explaining it to a neighbor.
4. **Review.** Read the summary and confirm the beneficiary and web link. The
   button creates a private draft; it does not publish or send anything.

After the draft is created, the full editor lets you add dates, phone goals,
images with alt text, structured story blocks, a promotion toolkit, and review
notes. The editor saves changes automatically and also has a **Save now** button.

## Writing examples

Prefer:

> **Every phone can help a local family stay connected.** Donate an unused
> phone and help our community keep access to school, work, and loved ones.

Avoid:

- guaranteed dollar amounts or promises about resale value;
- technical claims you cannot verify;
- pressure such as “act now or people will suffer”; and
- donor instructions that ask for a phone passcode, password, or backup code.

Use the story field for the human reason behind the campaign. Use the later
promotion toolkit for email, social, flyer, and QR-copy variations.

## What happens after setup

- **Editing:** you and permitted teammates can change the private draft.
- **Partner review:** a partner editor or administrator confirms the content.
- **Staff review:** Donate by Mail checks the exact revision, beneficiary,
  assets, and campaign terms.
- **Publication:** only the approved revision becomes public.

If staff sends feedback, it appears in the workspace beside the affected
revision. You can revise the working draft or restore an earlier revision; the
published page remains unchanged until a new exact revision is approved.

## If you get stuck

- A red message should explain the next action. Keep the page open; your
  answers remain in the wizard.
- If a web link is already used, add a season, year, or short organization name.
- If the nonprofit is missing, contact Donate by Mail rather than creating a
  duplicate beneficiary.
- If an image will not upload, use JPG, PNG, or WebP under the displayed size
  limit and add one sentence of alt text describing what the image shows.
- If another teammate changed the draft, reload before continuing so you do
  not overwrite their work.

For access or review questions, use the support contact supplied in your
partnership invitation. Never send a password, one-time link, phone passcode,
or backup code by email.
