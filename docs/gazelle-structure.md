# Gazelle-informed information architecture

## What was studied

- Gazelle separates its broad buy storefront from its trade-in experience.
- The trade-in landing page uses one dominant action, a short process explanation, device-category routes, confidence content, and help links.
- Device pages establish a visible sequence: select, offer, payment, and shipping.
- Help content is separated by task and answers practical eligibility, condition, carrier, security, and shipping questions.

## What Donate by Mail adopts

- One primary donor action on the homepage.
- A dedicated donation page rather than a full estimator and donor form embedded on the homepage.
- A four-stage donation workflow: phone, estimate, donor details, documents.
- Separate preparation and receipt pages so instructions appear when they are relevant.
- Clear paths for individual donors and nonprofit partners.

## What Donate by Mail intentionally does differently

- The tone is nonprofit and public-interest rather than promotional resale.
- The estimate is explicitly separated from tax valuation.
- The final acknowledgment is not presented as valid until physical receipt is verified.
- Controls and body copy are larger and more explicit for an audience that includes many donors over age 40.
- The design avoids glossy cards, aggressive urgency, and crowded category navigation.

## Page responsibilities

- `/`: mission, three-step overview, reassurance, and audience routing.
- `/donate-phone.html`: the complete staged donation workflow.
- `/prepare-phone.html`: backup, account sign-out, activation locks, cards, reset, and model-finding guidance.
- `/receipts.html`: packing slip, estimate, acknowledgment, and tax-value distinctions.
- `/for-nonprofits.html`: campaign partnership information.
- `/how-it-works.html`: full operational process.
- `/resources.html`: FAQ and help.
- `/about.html`, `/transparency.html`, `/contact.html`, and policy pages: organizational trust and compliance.
