A calm first step

Create a campaign without technical setup.

The nonprofit workspace asks four plain-language questions. You can start with rough wording, save a private draft, and invite a teammate later. Nothing becomes public from the first setup screen.

01

Name the campaign

Choose a name supporters will recognize, such as “Spring phone drive.” The workspace checks the web link for you.

02

Choose the verified beneficiary

Select the nonprofit that the campaign supports. If it is missing, ask your Donate by Mail contact to finish verification.

03

Tell supporters why it matters

Write a headline, a short explanation, and a few honest sentences. You can leave dates, images, and the phone goal for later.

04

Review your private draft

Confirm the name and beneficiary, then create the draft. Partner and Donate by Mail staff review the exact page before publication.

Helpful rule: use simple, accurate language. Never promise a fixed return or ask supporters for a phone passcode.

The campaign flow

A supporter should be able to understand it in a minute.

01

You invite supporters to participate

Use provided campaign language as a starting point for email, web, social, print, or event outreach.

The strongest message is usually simple: an unused phone can become funding for the cause.
02

Supporters complete the donation flow

They describe their phones, prepare their data, select the nonprofit, print the Donate By Mail address label, and pay their chosen carrier for postage.

Your staff does not need to collect device passwords, estimates, or technical details.
03

Donate by Mail receives and processes the phones

Devices are identified, inspected, and directed toward the appropriate reuse, resale, parts, or responsible recycling path.

Actual results depend on the devices that arrive, their condition, and the market at the time of processing.
04

You receive campaign reporting and proceeds

Each participating charity's proceeds share and eligible costs are defined in its approved campaign terms. Reporting reflects the phones actually received and processed.

No fixed campaign return is guaranteed before the phones are received and inspected.

A good pilot fit

Start with a group that already trusts you.

A pilot can work especially well when your organization has an engaged email list, alumni network, membership base, volunteer community, faith community, workplace supporters, or event audience.

  • You have a clear reason to ask supporters to participate.
  • You can reach people more than once during the campaign.
  • You want a fundraising option that does not ask every supporter for another cash gift.
  • You value transparent reporting over a promised headline number.

The supporter experience matters

We want your campaign to feel easy, not technical.

The donor sees plain-language instructions, a clear charity selection, and one step at a time. The goal is to make giving an old phone feel closer to mailing a package than managing an electronics program.
