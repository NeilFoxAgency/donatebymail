Why we exist

A phone can stop being useful to you long before it stops having value.

Upgrades, carrier changes, forgotten drawers, cracked screens, and old backup devices leave many phones sitting unused. Some still have resale value. Others may have useful parts. Devices that cannot be reused should be handled responsibly rather than left indefinitely or thrown into ordinary trash.

Donate by Mail connects that unused technology to nonprofit fundraising through a guided mail-in process designed for ordinary donors—not electronics experts.

Our model

Make the donor experience simple. Keep the money story transparent.

01

A donor starts with an unused phone

The donor describes the device, chooses the nonprofit they want to support, and follows clear preparation and mailing instructions.

The program is designed around phones people already own—not asking for another cash gift.
02

Donate by Mail handles the device workflow

Phones are received, identified, inspected, and directed toward reuse, resale, parts, or responsible recycling based on what actually arrived.

Physical receipt and inspection matter more than optimistic pre-mail estimates.
03

Useful value becomes charitable funding

For participating campaigns, the applicable proceeds share and eligible costs are defined in the campaign's approved terms. Results depend on the phones received and processed.

Reports should distinguish gross proceeds, costs, net proceeds, and partner distributions.

What we want to be known for

Four operating principles.

Plain language

Donors should understand estimates, receipts, preparation, and next steps without decoding industry jargon.

Data respect

We never need a donor's password or passcode and encourage people to back up and remove account access before mailing.

Measured claims

We distinguish projections from verified outcomes and avoid presenting estimates as guaranteed proceeds or tax values.

Responsible outcomes

Reuse and resale are preferred when practical; devices that cannot be reused should move toward responsible electronics recycling.
