Who can host one?

A phone drive works best inside a group that already communicates with its members.

Workplaces

Employees can bring unused personal phones during a defined collection window.

Faith & community groups

Congregations, neighborhood groups, and civic organizations can organize around a simple shared ask.

Clubs, events & school communities

Existing meetings and events create a natural reminder and collection point when an adult organizer coordinates the drive.

Nonprofit supporters

A phone drive can complement a broader supporter campaign when the nonprofit wants a local or event-based collection option.

A simple drive plan

Agree on the logistics before the first phone is collected.

01

Contact Donate by Mail

Tell us who is organizing the drive, the group you plan to invite, the approximate timing, and whether you expect a handful of devices or a larger collection.

Do this before publishing collection dates or promising a particular shipping arrangement.
02

Choose the collection model and time window

Depending on the campaign, people may be directed through the individual donation flow or an approved centralized collection plan may make more sense. We will clarify that before launch.

One clear deadline usually works better than an open-ended box that becomes permanent storage.
03

Give participants the preparation instructions

Every donor should back up needed files, sign out of accounts, disable activation locks, remove SIM and memory cards, and reset the phone when possible.

Never collect passwords, passcodes, or written account credentials.
04

Close the drive and follow the agreed shipping plan

Count and secure the collected devices, separate anything with a battery-safety concern, and follow the packaging or mailing instructions agreed to for the drive.

Do not improvise shipment of visibly damaged lithium-ion batteries.

What we can help with

You organize the people. We help make the phone part understandable.

  • Starter outreach language and a clear explanation of the purpose.
  • Links to donor preparation, data-safety, and device-identification guidance.
  • Current guidance on which devices and conditions need special attention.
  • An agreed collection, identification, and shipping approach for the specific drive.
  • For approved nonprofit campaigns, a reporting framework for devices received and resulting proceeds.

Three things never go in the collection box

Passwords, passcodes, and visibly unsafe batteries.

Participants should prepare their own phones before handing them over. If a phone is swollen, leaking, punctured, or unusually hot, set it aside and contact Donate by Mail instead of placing it with the other devices.
