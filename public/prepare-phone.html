Before you mail

Prepare your phone without losing what matters.

Take these steps before the phone leaves your hands. The goal is simple: keep your photos, messages, accounts, and phone number where they belong—with you.

Five steps

Work from top to bottom.

If a step does not apply to your phone, move on. If you get stuck, stop before erasing anything and use the help links below.

  1. Back up what you want to keep. Save photos, contacts, notes, messages, documents, and authenticator access to iCloud, Google, a computer, or another trusted backup. Confirm you can open the backup or sign in on another device.
  2. Make sure your important accounts work somewhere else. If you use the phone for two-factor authentication, banking, email, or password recovery, move that access before you reset the device.
  3. Sign out and remove account locks. Sign out of Apple or Google accounts and turn off Find My or the corresponding Android device protection. This separates the phone from your account before it changes hands.
  4. Remove physical SIM and memory cards. Keep your SIM card and removable storage. Do not place passwords, PINs, recovery codes, or written account information in the package.
  5. Factory reset only after the earlier steps are complete. Use the phone's built-in erase process. If the phone is too damaged to reset, describe that honestly in the donation flow instead of risking further damage.

Official device guidance

Use Apple or Google instructions when the menus do not match.

Phone menus change over time. These official manufacturer guides are the safest place to check current reset and account-removal steps.

iPhone

Apple recommends backing up, signing out, and using Erase All Content and Settings when preparing an iPhone for a new owner.

Apple: what to do before you give away or trade in an iPhone

Android

Google recommends backing up first, removing Google accounts, and then using the device's factory-reset controls. Exact menu names can vary by manufacturer.

Google: reset an Android device to factory settings

If you do not know the model

You usually only need the phone itself.

Find an iPhone model

Open Settings, choose General, then About. Look for Model Name and Capacity. On some older devices, a model number may also appear on the back or SIM tray.

Find an Android model

Open Settings and look for About phone or About device. Look for the model name, model number, and storage. The wording varies by Samsung, Google, Motorola, and other manufacturers.

If the phone will not turn on

Do not force it to power up or charge if the battery appears swollen, leaking, punctured, or unusually hot. Contact Donate by Mail before shipping a visibly damaged battery.

What should stay out of the box

Keep SIM cards, memory cards, passwords, passcodes, written recovery codes, earbuds, cases, and unrelated accessories unless Donate by Mail specifically asks for them.

A good place to stop and check

Not sure whether you erased everything correctly?

That is a good reason to wait before mailing. Review the FAQs or email us with the phone make and model. Never send us your password or passcode.
