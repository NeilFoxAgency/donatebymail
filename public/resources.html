Getting started

Before you begin

What kinds of phones can I donate?

The donation flow is designed for cell phones and smartphones. Describe the condition honestly, including phones that do not power on. If a battery is visibly swollen, leaking, punctured, or overheating, contact Donate by Mail before shipping it.

Can I donate more than one phone?

Yes. The donation flow supports multiple phones in one packet, so you can clear out several old devices at once.

What if I do not know the exact model?

Use the best information you have. On most phones, the model and storage are listed under Settings and About phone or General and About. Our preparation page has more specific directions.

Do I receive cash for my phone?

No. Donate by Mail is a charitable donation program, not a trade-in marketplace. The phone's value may help generate funding for the nonprofit selected in the donation flow.

Preparing your phone

Data and account safety

Should I factory reset the phone?

Yes, when possible—but only after you have backed up anything you want to keep, confirmed important account access elsewhere, signed out, and removed activation locks.

Do you need my passcode or password?

No. Do not mail or email passwords, passcodes, recovery codes, or written account credentials. Donate by Mail does not need them.

Should I remove my SIM card?

Yes. Keep physical SIM cards and removable memory cards. They may contain account or personal information and are not needed for the donation.

What if the phone is too damaged to reset?

Do not take unnecessary risks trying to make a damaged phone work. Describe the condition in the donation flow. If the battery looks swollen, leaking, punctured, or unusually hot, contact us before mailing.

Mailing and processing

Once the phone is ready

How do I mail the phone?

Complete the donation flow first. It creates the donation record and shows the available mailing option. Power the phone off, keep it from moving inside the package, and include the donation identification with the shipment.

What happens when the package arrives?

Donate by Mail records the physical receipt, confirms what arrived, and compares the devices with the donor description before finalizing donation documents or downstream processing.

What happens to the phone after inspection?

The next step depends on condition, safety, account locks, parts value, and market demand. A device may be reused, resold, used for parts, or responsibly recycled.

Can a damaged phone still help?

Sometimes. A phone with little resale value may still have parts value or need responsible electronics recycling. Actual results can only be known after the device is received and inspected.

Charity and documents

Where the donation goes

Can I choose which nonprofit my phone supports?

Yes. The donation flow lets you search the nonprofit directory and select one organization. Review the identifying details shown before you continue.

Is the value estimate my tax deduction?

No. The estimate is informational and is not an appraisal or tax valuation. Donate by Mail does not assign a fair market value to the property you donate.

When is my charitable acknowledgment final?

Only after Donate by Mail physically receives and verifies the donated property. A packing slip or preview created before receipt is not a final charitable acknowledgment.

How much reaches the selected charity?

For participating charity campaigns, 50% of net proceeds from sold phones is directed to the charity partner after applicable shipping, processing, resale, marketplace, and program costs. The actual amount depends on the devices received and the results of processing and resale.

Still holding the phone?

You do not need every answer before you start.

The donation flow is staged so you can describe the phone first and work through the remaining decisions one at a time.
